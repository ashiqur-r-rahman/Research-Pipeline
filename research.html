Question → Publication

The research pipeline, mapped end to end

Twelve stages from a raw idea to an accepted paper, with the tools that actually earn their place at each stage — not a link dump, a workflow.

Stage marker Time estimate Tool link
§01

Clarify the question

1–3 days
  • Define one focused, answerable question — not a topic.
  • Check it's original: has someone already answered this?
Quick scan
Google Scholar ↗
Free, broad index of papers, citations, and author profiles — first pass on any question.
Semantic Scholar ↗
AI search with one-line summaries and cleaner citation graphs.
Consensus ↗
Synthesizes 200M+ papers into a quick read on where the evidence stands.
§02

Broad literature discovery

3–7 days
  • Cast a wide net across the field before narrowing.
  • Explore outward from 2–3 seed papers.
Discovery
ResearchRabbit ↗
Free visual citation maps that grow from your seed papers — built for exploring a field.
Connected Papers ↗
Interactive graphs of similar and influential papers by citation network.
Elicit ↗
Deep, multi-step search for hard-to-find papers a keyword search would miss.
NCBI / PubMed ↗
Free U.S. government archive of biomedical literature and genomic data.
§03

Deep review & synthesis

1–3 weeks
  • Read, extract, and organize key findings by theme.
  • Cross-check that key citations still hold up.
Synthesis
SciSpace ↗
Chat directly with PDFs; summarize and pull data out of 280M+ papers.
Elicit ↗
Builds comparison tables of methods, results, and limitations across papers.
Scite ↗
Shows whether a paper's claims are actually supported or contradicted elsewhere.
Consensus ↗
Maps where the evidence across studies agrees or disagrees.
Zotero ↗
Free reference manager — folder everything by theme and tag as you go.
§04

Identify the gap

  • State plainly: what's known, what's missing, what you'll add.
  • Write it as a one-page research-gap memo.
§05

Design the methods

  • Choose methods, data sources, sample, and analysis plan.
  • Pre-register experimental work; secure ethical approval if required.
§06

Collect / generate data

  • Run experiments, surveys, simulations, or secondary analysis.
  • Keep raw data and scripts organized from day one — future you will look here first.
§07

Analyze the data

  • Clean, analyze, visualize with the right statistical or computational method.
  • Document every analytical decision as you make it.
§08

Write the first full draft

  • Write in this order: Methods → Results → Discussion → Introduction → Abstract & Title last.
Drafting
SciSpace AI Writer ↗
Drafts and polishes academic language section by section.
Zotero ↗
Insert citations directly from your library — never type a reference by hand.
§09

Self-review & strengthen

  • Check that every claim is actually backed by cited evidence.
  • Ask: would a critical reviewer accept this as written?
Verification
Scite ↗
Re-verify that your key citations are solid before anyone else reads this.
§10

Get feedback

  • Share with a supervisor, colleagues, or a writing group.
  • Revise on it — this step usually drives the biggest quality jump in the whole process.
§11

Final polish

  • Proofread carefully; pair tools with a human check.
  • Format to the target journal's guidelines; prepare cover letter, highlights, graphical abstract.
§12

Submit → review → publish

  • Pick the journal that matches your scope and impact, not just prestige.
  • Respond to reviewers thoroughly and politely; proof once more after acceptance.
Publishing
Elsevier ↗
Major STM publisher — journals like The Lancet and Cell, plus ScienceDirect and Scopus.
One tool from the original list is left out on purpose: Sci-Hub bypasses publisher paywalls without authorization, which is copyright infringement in most jurisdictions — so it's not included here. For paywalled papers, try your institution's library access, the author's own site, or emailing the author directly for a copy.
